# NextStep practical and academic study curriculum

Published to the Cloudflare app on November 21, 2026. The curated library contains 12 practical courses with `study-*` IDs and seven academic introductions with `college-*` IDs. The four courses already in the database were preserved, making 23 catalog courses at publication.

This curated library contains **104 lessons, 246 questions, 19 capstones, and 27,740 words of lesson instruction**. Each practical course has four lessons and lesson checks, three baseline questions, and three different final questions. Each academic introduction has eight lessons and lesson checks, five baseline questions, and five different final questions. Every capstone includes a four-point self-review rubric. Suggested durations are estimates for the lessons and exercises; a longer project or later mentor meeting can take additional time.

## How a learner uses a course

1. Sign in at [NextStep](https://nextstep-techwomenaz.pages.dev/) and open **Learn**. Course visibility follows the access included in the learner’s membership plan.
2. Choose a course and complete **Starting assessment**. The first response is kept as the baseline.
3. Read the course’s four or eight lessons, try the examples, and answer each lesson check. Save the capstone in a local document, spreadsheet, or project file; it is a practical exercise, not an automatic file submission.
4. Complete **See your progress** to compare the final assessment with the starting point. Review the feedback on missed answers. AI feedback and optional personalized follow-up courses depend on plan access and the configured AI service.
5. In **Study with a mentor**, choose **Request a course mentor**. The request includes the course title, skills, and suggested discussion topic. Add availability and the questions you want to discuss before sending.

A course mentor request is an open request to program staff. It does not book a meeting or claim that a professional has accepted. Staff select a real mentor and confirm the meeting. If a learner already has an open request, the app directs them to that request instead of creating a duplicate. If the plan excludes new mentoring requests, staff can change the plan or offer another support route.

## Academic introductions

These seven courses introduce university-level subjects through original lessons and worked cases. They are independent study courses, not a full semester, an accredited qualification, or college credit. The readings link to free material from the named publishers; NextStep does not claim an affiliation or endorsement. Completion does not authorize forensic casework or psychological diagnosis. All cases and study datasets used in the lessons are explicitly fictional.

### Introduction to Forensic Science

ID: `college-forensic-science` · Eight lessons · approximately 240 minutes plus optional readings and mentor review

**Prerequisites:** Basic secondary-school science, reading tables, and careful note-taking; no laboratory experience required.

**What learners practice**

- Turn a forensic claim into a testable question with competing explanations.
- Read a paper custody log and distinguish documented continuity from factual accuracy.
- Explain how transfer and persistence limit interpretations of trace and DNA evidence.
- Interpret controls, calibration, contamination risks, and measurement uncertainty.
- Evaluate pattern comparisons using documented features, validation, and possible error.
- Distinguish conditional probabilities and explain the value of independent review.
- Write a critical report that separates observations, interpretations, and limitations.

**Eight lessons**

1. Scope of Forensic Science: Testable Questions and Competing Explanations
2. Evidence Preservation and Chain-of-Custody Documentation
3. Trace Evidence: Transfer and Persistence
4. Analytical Measurement: Controls, Calibration, Contamination, and Uncertainty
5. DNA Evidence: Profiles, Mixtures, and Activity
6. Fingerprint and Pattern Comparison: Features, Validation, and Error
7. Human Factors and Evidence Interpretation
8. Capstone: Critical Report on a Fictional Case Packet

**Academic project:** A 250–400-word critical report using a supplied fictional trace-evidence packet, chain-of-custody log, measurements, controls, competing transfer explanations, and interpretation limits.

**Mentor check-in:** I am studying Introduction to Forensic Science. Please connect me with a mentor who can discuss the subject, review my academic project and reasoning, and suggest further study. I will bring my draft, the course readings, and two specific questions.

**Further reading:** [NIST: Forensic science](https://www.nist.gov/forensic-science), [NIST: Human factors in forensic science](https://www.nist.gov/forensic-science/human-factors-forensic-science).

## Staff workflow

- In **Courses**, open **Edit course** to change descriptions, lessons, answers, explanations, the mentor request prompt, or further-reading links. Use **Edit with AI** to revise the draft in plain language, then review it before saving.
- Use **Try as a learner** to check wording and assessment flow without creating a real learner record or result.
- For plans that include all courses, new published courses become available automatically. For plans that include selected courses, add the desired titles in the plan’s access settings.
- Review incoming mentoring requests and use the stated course, interests, and project needs when selecting a real mentor. Confirm availability and capacity before scheduling.
- During a mentor check-in, ask the learner to demonstrate the capstone, explain one decision, discuss one difficulty, and choose a next action. Record the real meeting and outcome after it occurs.
- Existing learners retain the course version they started. Editing a published course changes the version for new enrollments. Archiving hides it from new enrollments while preserving existing work.

## Measurement and content notes

The practical course baseline and final quizzes each contain three questions; the academic introductions contain five. These are short checks, not full university examinations. A percentage change describes performance on these small checks; it is not proof of professional competence or program impact. Pair it with the capstone, the learner’s explanation, actual meeting records, and later follow-up. Rubrics are learner/mentor review aids, not automatically graded outcomes.

The exercises and examples are original teaching material. Fictional datasets and people are labeled in the lessons. The linked primary sources are optional further reading, not claims of endorsement, accreditation, mentor availability, or completed research studies. No real participant records, credentials, or private workplace data are needed for the exercises.

Content review checked all lesson and assessment text, answer indices, distinct baseline/final questions, numerical examples, capstone rubrics, and mentor discussion prompts. All four Python worked examples were executed and produced their documented outputs. Reading links were checked; legacy CareerOneStop links and a redirected CISA file were replaced with working primary-source pages.
